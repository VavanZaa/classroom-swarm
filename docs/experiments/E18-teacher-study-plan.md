# E18 — Study-specific teacher comparisons and setup adjustment

The user authorized study-specific comparisons and nudging setup parameters to obtain similar directions to the studies. This experiment preserves the A30 engine and every historical source lock. It explicitly distinguishes a reference setup, an exploratory development search, selection using development outcomes, and fresh-seed confirmation. Selection is **directional fitting to already known study findings**, not independent human validation. No learning rule is changed; alpha remains the assumed trajectory value 0.0584. Null and contrary candidates are retained.

## Evidence and represented interventions

[Krueger's Project STAR paper](https://www.nber.org/papers/w6051) reports a randomized comparison of small classes (13–17) and regular classes (22–25), with higher test outcomes in small classes. Use N=15 versus 24, one teacher per class and the same total teacher budget. Do not convert percentile-rank findings into model exam percentage points. A resource-scaled sensitivity holds the ratio of available appointment time to students constant. The model omits classroom disruption, demographic heterogeneity and aides.

[Duflo, Dupas and Kremer (2011)](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/sites/default/files/research-paper/39%20Peer%20Effects%2C%20Teacher%20Incentives%2C%20and%20the%20Impact%20of%20Tracking%20Project.pdf) randomized achievement tracking across 121 Kenyan schools and reported gains in both starting halves. Table 1 reports roughly 42–44 students per post-split class. Use the same 84-student cohort under two 42-student mixed classes and two 42-student tracked classes, with one separate A30 teacher per class. Tracking follows a recorded baseline assessment, not hidden competence. Equal total school teacher resources are used in both arms. Adaptive instruction follows observed class assessments; teacher incentives, heterogeneous teacher effort, topic-specific gains and the full 18-month school intervention are not represented. Matching both halves is the target, not merely a positive school average.

The Sudoku peer-teaching study is a different intervention: it has no teacher. Its previously reported mixed-group advantage is not a contradiction of Kenyan teacher-adaptation effects. E18 does not relabel a teacher simulation as that lab experiment.

## Population, observations and clock

Both families use 12 homework episodes, 12 ticks per episode, 12 homework items and 100 unaided exam items. Exams occur every episode; feedback delay is zero. All student parameters and distributions originate in the strict A30 configuration template, with neutral confidence/benefit priors 0.5, effort aversion 0.15, and constant learning efficiency 0.0584. Student AI and answer copying are disabled. Peer interaction is disabled in the main teacher-mechanism comparisons; an ER degree-six peer sensitivity is added for the selected tracking setup.

STAR knowledge has exactly mean 0.45 and population SD 0.10 at both class sizes, using centered/scaled uniform quantiles, independently permuted with a declared semantic stream. This is a controlled population distribution, not a fitted kindergarten competence distribution. Kenya starts with 84 uniform-quantile competence values spanning 0.2–0.8 (a narrower 0.3–0.7 candidate is explicit). A baseline 100-item test is drawn from each student's initial competence. Lower/higher subgroups are the bottom/top 42 **baseline-assessment ranks**, fixed before assignment and outcomes. Mixed assignment randomly permutes the entire cohort and splits it into two equal-size classes; tracked assignment separates the baseline-assessment halves. The teacher's declared initial assessment prior is the mean recorded baseline score in its assigned class. This uses observed measurements, not true current competence.

Teacher observations subsequently use released exams, coverage one, no added noise/delay, update rate 0.5. Initial student traits are supplied as exact declared values. Graphs stay static. Semantic streams 131 (baseline measurement), 132 (mixed assignment) and 133 (population permutation) supplement unchanged A30 streams. Section-local student random draws are shared across paired conditions by section seed and local id; reassigning students changes which cohort member receives a local draw. Class-size changes also change tensor dimensions. Pairing is at the replicate/school level, not identical random trajectories for each global student. These limitations affect variance, not permission to expose hidden knowledge to the policy.

## Reference and candidate search

STAR reference: teacher budget 8, three-tick adaptive instruction followed by one-tick guidance, round-robin individual allocation, knowledge 0.95, skill one, learning multiplier one, guidance strength 0.1, confidence guidance rate 0.2; grading/admin costs zero to isolate instruction/attention. Candidates separately change guidance strength to 0.2 or 0.3, confidence rate to zero, action list to guidance only or explanation only, and budget to 12. A short-explanation candidate builds on explanation-only by changing duration from three to two ticks. Every movement is shown relative to its specified parent. No candidate changes the N=15 versus N=24 treatment definition or the baseline mean/SD.

Kenya reference: one three-tick adaptive broadcast per homework, budget 3, same teacher quality/learning multiplier, mean recorded class score as initial prior, observed exams thereafter. Candidates independently change readiness band 0.5 to 0.3 or 0.8, target offset 0 to 0.1, teacher learning multiplier 1 to 2, initial span 0.6 to 0.4, or add returned-homework observations. These are mechanism/setup sensitivities, not measured human coefficients. The readiness and multiplier changes alter assumed learning physics numerically without altering its rule; any agreement conditional on them must be labeled fitted/exploratory.

Use 12 development replicates for the full eight STAR and seven Kenya candidates: 528 classroom runs. Lock development seed 10061801 and confirmation seed 10061802, all sources/recipes/analysis rules and both exogenous cohorts before outcomes. Freeze the following selection rule: require positive development knowledge and exam differences (both lower and higher halves for Kenya). Among eligible candidates choose the **fewest declared changes from reference**, then the highest minimum exam effect, then candidate name. If none qualify, retain the best minimum-exam candidate and explicitly report that development did not achieve the target. Selection never sees confirmation outcomes.

## Fresh-seed confirmation and interpretation

After writing a hashed development review and selection record, run 60 fresh replicates for reference and selected setups (deduplicate if identical). STAR also includes teacher-disabled controls and a guidance-only equal-time-per-student control: budgets 5/8 for N=15/24. Kenya also includes fixed curriculum at 0.5 under the selected setup and selected setup with peer interaction enabled. These comparisons help distinguish teacher capacity/adaptation from population and peer effects. A resource-scaled control is not an algebraic null because appointment positions can still fragment students' available practice time.

Report final unaided exam, competence/gain, completed practice, teacher attendance, actual instructional difficulty, usable content, teacher content gain and received guidance/time. The primary outcome family has three selected effects: STAR small minus regular overall, Kenya tracked minus mixed in the lower half and in the higher half. Use paired replicate bootstrap with 3,000 resamples, pointwise 95% intervals and Bonferroni simultaneous 95% intervals for these three effects. Reference/control/sensitivity comparisons use descriptive pointwise intervals. Simulation intervals measure Monte Carlo uncertainty, not human sampling uncertainty. All contrasts move one setting or explicitly declare their treatment/assignment bundle. Encoded nonnegative content gain, priority/capacity rules and encouragement bonuses are identities; net outcome signs remain assessed results.

The target is positive exam and competence effects in all three selected contrasts. A direction can agree while its interval includes zero; distinguish positive descriptive means, pointwise support and simultaneous support. Fresh simulation seeds assess repeatability after selection, not whether the selected assumptions describe real classrooms. No additional candidate search after confirmation; any further exploratory round gets a new declared run identity and untouched seed pool.

Save and hash raw tables, resolved per-class settings, cohort membership/baseline assessments and run inventories. Re-read every classroom output and run A30 audits. Preserve damaged partial output before regeneration; never overwrite verified runs silently. Save results, all development candidates, selected assumptions and mismatches in the final report.
