# E19 — Bounded teacher setup revision after E18

The user authorized study-specific comparisons and setup nudging. E18's fresh seeds did not confirm the complete class-size and tracking pattern: STAR was near zero; Kenyan lower starters improved but upper starters' exam effect was uncertain. All E18 sources, data, choices and its failure report are preserved. E19 is an explicitly outcome-informed follow-up, using the unchanged A30 engine. It is not an independent test of the human literature.

Human anchors remain [Project STAR](https://www.nber.org/papers/w6051), with a positive smaller-class test direction, and [Duflo, Dupas and Kremer's Kenyan tracking experiment](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/sites/default/files/research-paper/39%20Peer%20Effects%2C%20Teacher%20Incentives%2C%20and%20the%20Impact%20of%20Tracking%20Project.pdf), with gains across baseline-achievement levels. Model sizes are 15/24 students and two 42-student sections respectively. The same school's 84 students are assigned randomly or by noisy baseline-test ranks. Each section has one teacher; school resources are equal across tracking assignments. Mean-based observed adaptation approximates one instructional mechanism; field teacher incentives/effort and classroom disruption remain absent.

## Frozen candidate panel

STAR reference retains E18: 12 episodes of 12 ticks, teacher budget 8, instruction then individual guidance, round-robin allocation, observation-based adaptive target. Its guidance strength is 0.1 and decay 0.5. Candidates: strength 0.3 or 0.5; strength 0.3 with confidence adjustment disabled, decay 0.8, or an 18-tick day; individual explanations replacing the action list, lasting two ticks, with learning multiplier 2 or 4. Action-list/duration/productivity combinations are bundles and cannot identify each part. A stronger or more productive intervention is a hypothesis, not a measured property of STAR.

Kenya reference retains E18's selected teacher learning multiplier 2, three-tick broadcast instruction, 12-tick day, readiness band 0.5, teacher knowledge 0.95, knowledge population mean 0.5 and span 0.6. Candidates: readiness 0.8 or 1.0; multiplier 4 alone or combined with each wider readiness band; teacher knowledge 1; target offset -0.05; population mean 0.45 or 0.4 retaining span 0.6. A mean shift changes the entire population, not an isolated peer effect. Initial scalar knowledge has no established conversion to the study's measured achievement distribution. Readiness and teacher productivity are unmeasured fit parameters. Wider readiness means a student can use more above-level content; it does not grant knowledge without completed correct, novel teaching.

The full panel (8 STAR, 10 Kenya profiles) is specified in `scripts/e19_teacher_studies.py`. Sources, semantic streams, seeds, initial cohorts and this plan are hashed before outcomes. Development uses 20 paired replicates, seed 10061911; fresh confirmation uses 80, seed 10061912. Both pools are declared now. Semantic baseline streams 131–133 and A30 action streams retain their meanings. Pairing occurs at classroom/school replicate; tracked students change local slots and therefore do not have perfect individual-level common random numbers.

## Selection and confirmation

For each study, require every target group's knowledge/gain and exam pointwise lower 95% bootstrap bounds to exceed zero, with every target exam mean at least +0.15 model percentage points. The minimum mean is a numerical fitting gate, not a human meaningful-effect threshold. Among eligible profiles choose the fewest changed keys, then the highest minimum exam lower bound, then name. If none qualify, select the highest minimum exam lower bound, mark development target unmet, and report failure rather than assume success. All candidates and intervals remain visible. Search multiplicity makes development intervals unsuitable as independent evidence.

After development analysis, hash the selection/review before any confirmation outcomes. Confirmation cannot reselect. Report final unaided exam and latent knowledge/gain, paired pointwise intervals and a simultaneous Bonferroni three-exam family (STAR overall, Kenya lower, Kenya upper). Bootstrap uses 3,000 resamples, seed 10061999. These intervals measure simulator Monte Carlo variation, not human uncertainty.

Confirmation includes reference and selected settings; selected STAR with teacher disabled; selected STAR with zero learning multiplier, guidance strength and confidence update, retaining action durations/calendar; E18's equal-guidance-time-per-student sensitivity; selected Kenya with fixed difficulty 0.5; and selected Kenya with voluntary peer access in static degree-six ER networks, copying disabled. Zero support removes multiple teacher benefit channels if guidance and content coexist; their effects are not separately identified. The equal-guidance sensitivity changes the action protocol and appointment fragmentation as well as the budget; it is not an exact null. The resource ratio is 5 guidance ticks/15 students versus 8/24. Report teacher contribution as the selected class-size contrast minus its corresponding teacher-disabled contrast using paired replicate differences; this helps detect random population/schedule effects. Fixed curriculum may remain sensitive to initial mean and is a diagnostic comparator, not the Kenyan control condition itself.

Every raw class log is saved, hashed, re-read, A30-audited and checked against its resolved settings, cohort IDs and observed baseline scores. Report content gain, lesson difficulty, teacher time, guidance and practice opportunity. Historical sources and E18 report sources are rechecked at each stage. No engine changes are authorized within this experiment; any defect would require a separately identified correction.

Encoded identities: teacher content gains are nonnegative; teacher guidance changes independent-practice utility; capacity bounds appointments. The sign of net learning/class-size/tracking differences is not encoded. No tick-to-human-minute conversion or numerical effect-size replication is claimed. Known study directions inform the fit; fresh seeds test repeatability only. Stop after this bounded panel and confirmation, preserving incomplete or opposite outcomes.
