# V4 audit, 25 September 2026

A read-only review of the whole V4 line (A22–A25, experiments E9–E12) for logical defects: mechanisms that impose what is claimed to emerge, controls that do not control, contrasts that cannot identify what they name, and claims that outrun their evidence. Ten items were found. Every quantitative statement below was measured against the locked parameters before it was written.

Disposition: **engine defects are fixed in the [[docs/15-v4-corrected-controls|A26 fork]] and re-run as [[docs/experiments/E13-results|E13]]**; reporting defects are corrected as errata on the affected results documents. No locked source, lock file or raw output was modified. A22–A25 remain byte-identical.

## 1 · Defects in the engines

### 1.1 The null-tutor control was unlearnable — FIXED in A26, measured in E13

`v4_public_ai.py` labelled an attempt `origin='ai_tutor_attempt'` only when the hint strength was positive, and `public_grade_credit` assigned tutor credit only through that origin. At `ai_hint_strength=0` no attempt was ever labelled, so the action was invisible to credit assignment. Measured on replicate 0 at E12's parameters: 0.5% of grade returns credited `ai_tutor` against 21.3% in `tutor_ai`, the perceived tutor value stayed at its initial prior (0.480 vs 0.547), no `ai_guided` route existed — yet 227 consultations still occurred against 256 in the working arm. E12's `tutor_ai0` row was therefore "a useless tutor nobody can find out is useless", not "a tutor with no effect".

A26 credits by hint **source**. E13 measures the old rule directly as `tutor_ai0_uncredited`: it shifts the exam by +0.19 points (95% CI +0.08 to +0.30) and homework by +1.20 (+0.22 to +2.23). Small in magnitude, wrong in meaning. With the control repaired, `tutor_ai0 − tutor_ai` becomes a real effect: −1.53 exam points.

### 1.2 `B_check` was written on every homework and read by nothing — errata for E9/E10, regression test added

`v4.py` updated all four benefit entries with credit `used/total`, but the policy reads only `benefits[0..2]`; index 3 appears nowhere except the log line. Because credit is normalised, the inert channel absorbed update mass from the channels that do drive choice. In the E10 reference run, checks are 31.8% of all benefit-credit mass, so the live beliefs moved about a third slower than they otherwise would — and the rate differed across E10's own conditions (19.09 checks in `reference`, 18.48 in `check0`, 0 in `no_check`).

Not present in the A25/A26 line, which has no checking action, so there is nothing to fix forward. A regression test in `tests/test_v4_corrected.py` now perturbs each benefit index and requires the score dictionary to change, so a write-only belief channel cannot recur. E9 and E10 carry errata.

## 2 · Claims entailed by their own encoding

### 2.1 A24/E11 — errata applied

`v4_grades.py` adds `grade_response * grade_shortfall` to the answer score, and E11's primary contrast is that coefficient against zero. "Adding bad-grade copying pressure raises late copied submissions from 11.45% to 17.73%" restates the rule with a magnitude attached. `docs/13` says so; the README and the results headline did not.

### 2.2 A25/E12 — errata applied

Answer AI emits packets at `ai_accuracy` and grants no knowledge, while grades come only from packet bits and knowledge only from attempts. Any positive uptake must raise homework and lower the exam. The bolded headline was an identity. The findings are uptake, substitution and propagation.

E13's preregistration names all three such identities in the lock file before the run, and the results document states them before any number derived from them.

## 3 · Contrasts that could not identify what they named — FIXED in E13

`public_no_ai − motivation_only` moved `peer_rule`, `distant_weight`, `closeness_spread`, `grade_bias` and `public_signal` in one step. `public_signal` is not neutral: under the strictly-higher-grade rule the eligible pool's mean grade always exceeds the requester's, so blending it in biases the perceived value of copying upward by construction. Eligibility and an upward payoff bias were bundled together.

`motivation_only − legacy` bundled the intrinsic learning bonus with the waiting bonus, with nothing separating them.

E13 replaces both with a one-parameter-per-rung ladder, and the prediction above is confirmed by measurement. `public_signal` is the dominant component under either ordering (+7.25 points of copying added first, +13.37 removed last, against a +14.39 total) and the only one that clears δ both ways. The eligibility filter is the striking case: added on its own it *reduces* copying by 2.35 points, but removed from the finished model it *increases* it by 1.51, because in that model the filter is exactly what guarantees every eligible source outscores the requester and so feeds the upward bias in `public_signal`. The two are not separable, which was the finding. The "motivation" step is entirely the learning bonus; the waiting bonus is a null. A post-hoc order check (`scripts/e13_order_robustness.py`) shows the components interact strongly, so no single ordering attributes the total — recorded in the E13 results rather than left implicit.

## 4 · Subgroup labels drawn from one arm of the contrast — FIXED in E13

`e10.py`, `e11.py` and `e12.py` derived early-attempt groups from `reference` / `public_no_ai` and applied them everywhere. A subgroup metric measured in the label-source arm is selected on that arm's own noise; the same metric elsewhere is not, so every `group_gap_*` difference carries a regression-to-the-mean term. Common random numbers keep E10's arms close enough that the bias is small; the protection is weaker in E12, where the label source differs sharply from `legacy` and `answer_ai`.

E13 contrasts subgroups defined by the exogenous learning trait α and keeps behavioural groups as a per-condition description that the analysis code excludes from the effect table. The two groupings do give different pictures of the same runs (high-minus-low final-knowledge gap under answer AI: 4.33 by trait, 7.51 by behaviour).

## 5 · Undeclared score-function assumptions — exposed and tested in E13

`docs/14` states the objective as "expected grade improvement minus personally weighted effort cost", but `attempt` and `hint` shrink their benefit toward confidence while `answer`, `ai_answer` and `ai_tutor` do not. Measured on replicate 0, the perceived attempt grade was 0.586 against a true unaided grade of ≈0.647 without AI, and 0.532 against 0.548 with answer AI — a systematic under-rating of own work of 2 to 6 points against a softmax temperature of 0.10. E13 exposes this as `confidence_weight` and finds the AI conclusions survive it (−0.82 to +0.96 exam points across w = 0 to 1).

Separately, all benefits are anchored at K₀ and `benefit_prior` was exercised only in E9. E13 finds it is the widest lever on **uptake** — direct AI use moves from 16.93% to 62.50%, a 45.6-point span against 43.3 for AI accuracy and 25.3 for effort aversion — and roughly tied with effort aversion on the **exam** (6.34 points against 6.05). An assumption nobody had varied matters about as much as the parameter the study was built around. The belief-channel audit shows why — under answer AI only 20.41% of returned grades credit the student's own attempt, so the belief about the abandoned option stops receiving evidence.

## 6 · Smaller items

**Late hints.** A peer hint was requestable with six ticks left, but service could be delayed three ticks, so hints could arrive too late for the attempt they were for — while the AI tutor already reserved that time. Measured: 7/85 hints at E9's parameters, 8/101 at E11's, 12.37% in E13's unguarded arm. A26 applies the same reservation to peer hints; E13 confirms a pre-stated null on outcomes (exam −0.05, −0.14 to +0.05) and a real change in the hint statistics.

**A23's belief alignment is imposed, not learned.** `align_check_value` is arithmetically exact, but nothing in the model learns that checking stopped teaching, so `check0` and `check0_unaligned` bracket "knows instantly" and "never finds out" with no learner in between. Errata on E10; superseded by A24 and not carried forward.

**E12 had no topology arm.** `e12.py` generated an ER graph every replicate and used it in no condition. E13 runs all three main arms on both families; the answer-AI contrast replicates to two decimals.

**A transcription slip.** `README.md` reported the E10 spread as rising "from 15.13"; `runs/E10/derived/condition_estimates.csv` gives 0.15124995, which is the 15.12 printed in `E10-results.md`. Corrected.

## Checked and found sound

The A24 parity test exists and constrains the right fields. Effort is priced per whole path at each decision and is not double-charged. `kappa` scales only the effort trait, leaving the `K0` and `alpha` draws untouched, so the effort arms are clean. Streams 106 and 107 are separately seeded and cannot perturb the historical tensors. The `access` term is a correct expected-value discount, not a double count of the reliability belief. Attempts can never be truncated by the deadline. E12's same-time homework/exam correction does what it claims. A25's `estimates` on receipt, packet provenance through peer forwarding, the one-consultation limit and the isolate-access guarantee all behave as specified.
