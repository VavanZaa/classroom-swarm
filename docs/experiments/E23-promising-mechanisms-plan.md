## Encoded identities and limitations

Payments, copied answers, exams and elapsed time grant no direct competence. Zero transfer blocks direct cross-domain learning; it does not prevent common followup changing participation. Fixed-curriculum tracking with no peer channel and identical student opportunities is an assignment identity, not evidence of no human tracking effect. Some single-block high starters begin with fully mastered foundation skill under the declared projection; their zero foundation gain is a ceiling identity. A new common skill test bank is distinct from A32's scalar measurement, so cross-model effect differences include measurement structure. Skill pretests provide more observed information than the original field baseline; report this assumption. Private confidence/benefit beliefs remain pooled across skills, dialogue is numerical, contact graphs static, and teacher policy greedy. Helper-refinement is skill-specific residual mastery and displaced time, not a fitted language/explanation model.
