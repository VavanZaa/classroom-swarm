## Motivation and uncertainty

Main cases use `grade_effort_only=True`, `learning_value=0`: remove the former intrinsic learning bonus and arbitrary positive waiting bonus. Students compare expected grade improvement against personal effort aversion times activity duration; waiting and submitting yield zero incremental utility. Grades and private success beliefs, rather than true K or current item correctness, inform choices. The previously requested bad-grade shortcut-pressure term remains an explicit behavioral bias toward peer/AI answer requests. This is bounded, imperfect grade-seeking, not an omniscient optimizer of future exam outcomes.

Effort aversion retains the inherited heterogeneous trait distribution with scenario parameter kappa=.15; controls use 0 and .5. Higher effort aversion penalizes attempts and tutoring more than a short AI answer. No student is assigned a "lazy" type, and no direct knowledge penalty is imposed. Low unaided performance must arise from omitted practice and subsequent feedback. AI true accuracy and true tutoring strength do not enter choice scores; the expected tutor benefit is the student's learned B_ai_tutor. Students can be wrong about an easy method's value. Returned homework grades reinforce beliefs; delayed unaided exam feedback can reveal a mismatch. An answer-AI control suppresses exam feedback to isolate that information channel.

## Information and source choice

`PublicAIParams(GradeParams)` and `simulate_public_ai` extend A24. Previous homework grades become public together at h+1. Current homework correctness and latent K remain hidden from decisions. Main `peer_rule='higher'`: answer candidates must have a strictly higher last returned grade than the requester. No peer copying in homework 1, because no previous grades exist. Equal grades are ineligible. A sensitivity `peer_rule='relaxed'` allows familiar lower-scoring sources; `legacy` ignores public grades for eligibility. This distinguishes a lower-scoring alternative among qualifying sources from a source below one's own grade.

The friendship graph remains fixed. A frozen pairwise closeness layer gives existing edges weight `edge_weight*(1+spread*(2U-1))`, spread=.5; other classmates have weak acquaintance weight=.1. Zero acquaintance weight restricts interaction to graph neighbors. This implements the user's closeness-based answer rather than an all-or-nothing friend/nonfriend assumption. Closeness is symmetric, exogenous, fixed and logged; only one's own row is used by a student. New semantic stream 106 initializes closeness; it cannot perturb other randomness. This A25 layer supersedes earlier neighbor-only eligibility but never rewires the historical graph or learns contact weights.

For eligible answer sources, selection weight is `closeness^relationship_strength * private_access * exp(grade_bias*public_grade)`, with relationship_strength=1, grade_bias=3. For hints there is no grade filter or grade factor. A close moderate scorer can outweigh a distant top scorer; no deterministic highest-grade selection exists. Learned availability still reflects only direct requests. Answer benefit estimates blend the private B_answer with the weighted mean public source grade, public_signal=.5. The received packet is estimated from the chosen source's public grade and B_answer, never its current item bits. These coefficients are assumptions, not fitted social preferences.

## AI interventions

Universal voluntary access from homework 2, `ai_mode='none'|'answer'|'tutor'`. No forced adoption, live LLM or outside API is used. Everyone can choose AI regardless of peer rank or network isolation. One AI consultation per student per homework; it consumes time and can be followed by other activities. AI has no peer congestion or willingness bottleneck.

- Answer AI: costs 1 tick and generates a complete packet with item correctness probability .9 (sensitivity .8 and 1). It adds no direct K. The student estimates its value using their own learned B_ai_answer, not the simulation's true accuracy. Bad-grade copying pressure also increases its score. A high returned AI-answer grade reinforces this action when above its prior expected benefit.
- Tutor AI: costs 2 ticks and supplies a hint of strength .25 (sensitivity 0 and .5). It supplies no answer and no immediate K. A subsequent 3-tick attempt consumes the hint and uses the existing guided-attempt rule. Tutor choice requires enough remaining time for consultation plus attempt. Whether the student actually follows through remains behavioral, not compulsory.

The two AI modes are separate conditions. Direct AI output and its peer-forwarded descendants retain exact item bits, IDs and external root=-1, with origin='ai_answer'. Peer copying never repairs/re-samples an answer. AI-tutored own attempts use a distinct origin label. Final route distinguishes direct AI, peer copying, own independent attempt and own guided attempt, so peer receipt of an AI packet credits peer copying, not personal AI use. AI consultation randomness uses new semantic item stream 107; all historical streams are untouched.

Private B and activity counts contain five actions: attempt, hint, answer, ai_answer, ai_tutor. Submitted AI answers credit AI answer; an AI-guided final attempt splits credit between attempt and AI tutor. Other A24 grade timing, copying pressure, confidence attribution, exam feedback and learning rules remain unchanged. No automatic checking study action returns.
