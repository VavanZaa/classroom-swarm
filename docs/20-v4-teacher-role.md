# A30 — Separate teacher, observed instruction and finite time

This user-authorized fork adds a teacher to A29 without changing historical engines or locks. `simulate_teacher(p, graph, draws, condition="declared", initial=None, teacher=None)` uses A29 student parameters and a separate `TeacherSettings` declaration. Disabled teachers delegate to A29 exactly, including all raw tables and the manifest. Enabled teachers use model identity `A30-v4.5-teacher-role`. N counts students only: the teacher's string identity is outside the student graph, traits, packets, exams and population averages.

## Information and allocation

The teacher maintains an assessment estimate per student, initialized from a declared prior. Homework enters its observation queue only after a grade has actually been returned; unaided diagnostic exams enter after the historical exam release date. An additional observation delay, coverage probability and clipped Gaussian measurement noise are configurable. No assessment is visible in its source homework. Assisted homework has a separately declared observation weight (default zero); a covered unaided assessment has weight one. Estimate update is `estimate += observation_learning_rate * weight * (observed_score - estimate)`. Missing signals cause no update. True assessment scores remain research records, separate from observed scores. Exam measurement itself grants no knowledge.

Allocation is lowest observed estimate first with previous service/id tie breaking, or round robin by previous service/id. The policy receives estimates, counts of effective observations and previous service only. It cannot access hidden current competence, actual item errors, private student beliefs, graph statistics or explanation correctness. Fixed instruction uses declared difficulty; adaptive instruction uses the mean observed estimate of invited students plus a declared offset, clipped to [0,1]. Corrective feedback requires a released, effective assessment and is a scaffold based on that assessment, not omniscient correction of current errors.

## Actions and schedule

An ordered list declares action priorities. Whole-class `instruction` is one broadcast charged once. `explanation`, `corrective_feedback` and `guidance` offer individual appointments in the allocation order, charged per appointment. Appointments that do not fit are logged unserved; they are offered again on later homeworks, with no stale appointment backlog. Declared participation draws determine attendance. A declined invitation still consumes its reserved teacher time, but consumes no student time and grants no learning.

Appointments share the homework clock. Participating students are unavailable during them; independent and peer activities must finish before their next appointment. The teacher can serve only one appointment at a time. Shared-practice protocols start after the teacher's last planned appointment; free students explicitly wait during this instructional phase. Shared practice that no longer fits is logged as displaced and grants no gain. This phase convention is a protocol assumption, not a discovery about optimal scheduling.

Teaching is completed active retrieval with an explanation. The A29 item physics uses frozen learner competence, declared teacher competence/skill, appointment difficulty and separate semantic retrieval/explanation draws. Correct, novel, usable content determines quality q. Its fractional gain is `min(1, alpha / attempt_learning_divisor * teacher.learning_multiplier * q)`; completed teaching grants no generic independent-attempt gain or teacher helper gain. Incorrect/inaccessible explanations, zero learning multiplier and absence yield zero teacher learning. It creates no homework answer packet: subsequent homework performance reflects changed competence through existing practice. Ordinary student learning and teacher learning are combined synchronously using residual multiplication and logged separately. Longer duration displaces other activity; it does not automatically increase the learning coefficient.

Guidance grants no direct knowledge and creates no answer. It blends student confidence toward the teacher's observed estimate by `guidance_confidence_rate`, and adds `guidance_strength * (1 - observed_estimate)` to a private recommendation bonus capped at one. The bonus decays by `guidance_decay` at each homework start and is read in independent-attempt choice scores. This explicitly assumed encouragement channel can affect practice; it is not a calibrated psychological or counselling model. Setting both guidance rates to zero is a time-only control.

## Workload and grading

One integer `time_budget` per homework must fit within student ticks. Administrative tasks arrive each homework and unfinished admin work carries forward. Admin has first priority, followed by due homework grading oldest homework/student first, followed by appointments. Grading costs `ceil(grading_ticks_per_student * (1 - grading_automation))` ticks per submission, including missing submissions. Automation affects workload only, not grade accuracy or observation noise. A zero cost preserves automatic next-homework return at tick zero, even with an admin backlog. With positive cost, ungraded submissions remain queued; student feedback/public grades and teacher homework observations arrive at the actual grading completion tick. Decisions before that tick cannot use them. The teacher plans appointments at the end of its reserved workload interval using signals available by that tick. Observation delays of one or more homeworks arrive at tick zero of the later homework. No reserved action is counted before completion. A final H+1 grading day returns what fits, with unresolved grading/admin/observation queues saved in raw logs. That closing day changes no student knowledge and offers no instruction.

This explicitly supersedes A24's free, universal next-homework grade timing **only in enabled A30**. Public source selection uses the latest actually returned grades; delayed teacher feedback can affect choices. Costly assessment administration, multiple teachers, topic-level error repair, rewards and relationship changes are outside A30. All numerical defaults are uncalibrated assumptions. Automation's reduction in required grading ticks and nonnegative completed-content learning are encoded identities, not empirical findings. Net learning effects can have either sign because appointments displace practice and incomplete grading delays feedback.

## Declaration, randomness and raw checks

`python -m classroom_swarm.teacher_config template --output configs/my-teacher.json` writes all settings, initial distributions, teacher settings and network declarations. `validate CONFIG` checks the resolved experiment; `run CONFIG --output NEW_DIRECTORY` locks sources/settings before execution. Conditions can override `teacher` independently; bundled contrasts must declare why they do not identify separate changes. Old A29 configuration commands remain unchanged.

Streams 126–130 are teacher observation coverage, noise, attendance, retrieval and explanation, indexed by seed/replicate/source homework or action kind/start/student. Broadcast content is shared among attendees. Historical student/graph/item/exam streams remain unchanged. Raw `teacher_identity`, `teacher_observations`, `teacher_observation_state`, `teacher_queue`, `teacher_actions`, `teacher_attendance`, `teacher_learning`, `teacher_guidance`, `teacher_workload`, `teacher_budget`, `teacher_backlog` and `teacher_displaced_practice` tables reconstruct decisions, completion, knowledge and all teacher/student time. Validators check bounds, information timing, observed-policy replay, workload conservation, no overlapping commitments, excluded teacher outcomes and learning reconstruction.

Required tests cover disabled parity, separate identity, broadcast charging, limited capacity, grading backlogs/automation, delayed/noisy/absent observations, assisted-score weighting, non-omniscient policy, usable content, attendance refusal, no direct guidance knowledge, a guidance bonus read by choices, shared-practice displacement, reproducibility, strict declarations and raw-validator corruption detection. Implementation diagnostics are functional checks; they do not establish agreement with a human study.
